The homepage: four directions, one recommendation.

Our current site is beautifully written but too dense for the person it was built for. This document presents four structural directions the board can choose from, one recommendation, and what shipping looks like in each case.

Prepared for the board · The Talking Point Group

Section 01 · The problem, honestly stated

Our current homepage doesn't serve the person it was built for.

The website we've built is editorial, warm and well-crafted. Every section rewards a reader who scrolls — funders, school administrators, HR directors. That reader exists. But that reader is not the person we exist for.

The person we exist for is someone whose executive function is impaired by depression, anxiety, trauma or grief — someone whose brain is in fog, who scrolls once and has already spent their capacity. Our homepage currently has eighteen sections stacked vertically. For that visitor, every section is a cost. It's not a design preference — it's a clinical concern. Cognitive overload for someone at the edge is real.

This document is the answer to: what should the front door of TTP look like, so the person who most needs us finds their way to help in the fewest possible clicks — without losing the trust signals a funder or school buyer needs to see?

Section 02 · The four structural directions considered

Four ways the front door could work.

Each option is a distinct philosophy — not a variation on the same theme. Each has a clear front-door metaphor, a real trade-off, and a precedent (or the lack of one) in the mental-health sector.

Option 01 Recommended

The Triage

Homepage is a decision the visitor makes in three seconds. One line. Three-to-six doors. Editorial content lives below the fold — visible only if the visitor chooses "learn more". Model: 988 Lifeline, Samaritans.

Option 02

The Conversation

The site is a WhatsApp thread. Reading the site is reading messages from TTP. No traditional sections at all. Radical, and requires deciding upfront who replies when someone types something serious.

Option 03

The Small Book

Nothing scrolls forever. Everything is a page you turn — bite-sized chapters, one idea per screen, deep calm by design. Preserves editorial voice. Slower for someone in acute crisis unless paired with a persistent help layer.

Option 04

The Choice

Two parallel sites behind one door. On arrival: "I'm here for me" (crisis-safe, minimal) or "I'm here to learn about TTP" (full editorial). Serves both audiences perfectly, but doubles the build.

Below the doors, the site keeps the strongest existing sections — trust, real humans, one case story, one closing call — and cuts the rest. Nothing precious is lost. It's just edited.

The sixth "Just say hello" door is the single most important addition. It's for the visitor who cannot decide which category they are — which is the visitor in the worst cognitive state. A door that removes the need to categorise your own crisis before you can ask for help.

Section 06 · What ships, and when

Three phases, four weeks, zero editorial content lost.

Every phase is reversible in one commit. No feature we've already built gets deleted — the editorial content stays live either on the homepage below the fold, or on the interior page it now belongs on.

Phase 01 · Week 1

Triage front door · persistent crisis layer

  • New hero: "You're not alone." + WhatsApp CTA + Find your door
  • Six-door grid including "Just say hello"
  • Persistent "Not okay right now?" strip on every page
  • Sticky WhatsApp + Quick Exit on mobile
  • Live-response badge on hero
Phase 02 · Week 2–3

Small Book pass on interior pages

  • Tonight, Someone-you-love, Schools, Service pages rebuilt one-idea-per-screen
  • No interior page carries more than four stacked sections
  • Language capability tag on every team member card
  • Consolidated trust: HPCSA · POPIA · SLA-bound · Clinical escalation
Phase 03 · Week 4

Polish · inclusion · accessibility

  • 11-language switcher on every page
  • Multilingual pre-filled WhatsApp messages
  • Formal WCAG 2.2 accessibility audit and fixes
  • Emergency-lines footer column (SAPS · GBV · SADAG · Ambulance)
  • Performance / load-time pass — critical on mobile networks
Section 07 · What we are deliberately trading

The trade being made — eyes open.

The honest trade

This recommendation deliberately trades some editorial impact on the homepage for user safety. A first-time funder or school administrator will see a triage screen with clear trust markers, and might scroll to learn more — but they will not land on a full editorial magazine. That is the trade. It is the right trade — for a mental-health organisation, the person in crisis has to come first — but the board should agree it is the trade being made.

Section 08 · The ask of the board

Three decisions, in order.

1. Do we accept the clinical premise? The current homepage is too dense for someone in acute distress, and that is a problem worth solving now.

2. Do we approve The Triage as the direction (Option 01), understanding that Options 02, 03 and 04 remain on the shelf as future possibilities?

3. Do we approve the four-week phased build, understanding that no existing content is lost — only moved, merged or moved to interior pages?

Prepared for the board of The Talking Point Group. Any of the alternative directions can be built to the same standard — this document simply argues why The Triage should be picked first.
